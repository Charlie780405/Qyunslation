Dupilumab-associated de novo vitiligo in a patient treated for severe asthma

●Dupilumab suppresses T helper (Th) 2 pathway 
via dual blockade of IL-4 and IL-13 receptors. 
●Suppression of Th2 pathways may contribute 
to overexpression of Th1 pathways and 
increased CD8+ cytotoxic T cell activity 
implicated in melanocyte destruction in vitiligo 
development.
○Other key immune cells hypothesized to 
promote vitiligo development include T 
regulatory, resident memory T & dendritic 
cells. 
●Cases of vitiligo development following 
initiation of dupilumab have been reported 
globally, especially within the first six months of 
initiation. 
●Upon vitiligo development, there is not a 
uniform recovery of melanocytes.
○Some patients recover pigmentation while 
continuing dupilumab treatment, while others 
do not recover pigmentation despite 
discontinuing dupilumab. 
○There are few reported cases of patients with 
previously-diagnosed vitiligo lesions that 
experience repigmentation upon initiation of 
dupilumab treatment; this should not be 
considered as standard.
●Presentation: 46-year-old male, Fitzpatrick skin 
type IV, with a history of severe asthma, atopic 
dermatitis (AD), and extensive sun exposure, 
presented to clinic with a 1-year history of severe 
sun sensitivity and new-onset, diffuse, 
ivory-colored patches.
○Started on dupilumab by his pulmonologist for 
moderate-to-severe asthma (history of ICU 
admission 2x) just before symptom onset.
○Significant asthma improvement on dupilumab; no 
other reported adverse effects.
●Medications: dupilumab pre-filled pen, albuterol.
●Physical Exam: revealed generalized, diffuse, 
near-universal ivory-colored patches of 
hypopigmentation. 
○Other dermatologic findings included 
erythematous eczematous plaques bilaterally on 
arms and face, solar lentigines, actinic keratoses, 
and nevi across the face, trunk, and extremities.
●Patient chose not to discontinue dupilumab given 
improvement of asthmatic exacerbations.
●Recommendation to mitigate depigmented lesion 
sun sensitivity by use of daily SPF 30+ and motor 
vehicle window tints; also started on roflumilast 
0.15% cream for AD.
●Dupilumab is a fully human monoclonal antibody 
that inhibits interleukin receptor signaling (IL-4 and 
-13).
●While generally very well-tolerated, reported 
adverse events include rebound atopic dermatitis, 
injection site dryness, eczema, and rarely, vitiligo. 
●Vitiligo is characterized by depletion of melanocytes 
within the epidermis and impaired melanocyte stem 
cell regeneration, leading to well-defined milky-white 
lesions on the skin.

Conclusions
●Even with dupilumab’s well-tolerated profile, 
longitudinal monitoring and open
